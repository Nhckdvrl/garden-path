# Choe, Yoshida & Cole (2022) — semantic-processing depth may reinforce wrong interpretations

[Published open-access research](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5d84n2x7), DOI 10.5070/G601136, Glossa Psycholinguistics. **Read scope 2026-10-12:** accessible publisher/search abstract and introduction excerpt; not full methods, statistical tables or audio stimuli. Mark PRIMARY_ABSTRACT/INTRO, not FULL.

## Problem/ancestry

Classic lingering GP errors could stem from a failed *structural rewrite*, or a correct new tree coexisting with a strongly processed *old event meaning*. Most designs change sentence structure or lexical plausibility and conflate both.

## Decisive move (from abstract, RECONSTRUCTED trajectory)

Use *auditory pitch accent* (focus cue) on the ambiguous verb vs a safe region. That can selectively deepen semantic interpretation of the **early incorrect verb–object relationship** without changing the source words or sentence grammar. Speeded comprehension then tests whether the wrong semantic interpretation is harder to reject.

**Reported:** Accent on the verb worsened accuracy without a comparable RT increase. This puts pressure on “everything is just late syntactic surprise” and supports stronger erroneous semantic elaboration as a possible cause. It is human auditory comprehension evidence, not a token-level LM belief or a model-free causal demonstration about latent representation.

## Novelty scale and our caution

Rather than create a new GN/GP metric, the study uses a **separate manipulation of semantic attention** to distinguish old-tree activity from early meaning consolidation. This is a valuable experiment-design pattern. But generic early-question/goal priming already appeared in SSN E65/E98 and had heterogeneous, sometimes adverse outcomes. A new text-LLM manipulation that merely highlights words or asks early questions is not novel and creates task-directive confounds.

If we consider it later, the clean research question would be whether semantic elaboration **changes the dependency footprint** beyond local error and affects cross-task use even after neutral re-reading. This must outperform ordinary foregrounding and attention/position baselines.
