# Chromý (2022) — When readers fail to form a coherent representation of garden-path sentences

[Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218211037152); Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology 75(1):169–190 (online 2021). **READ SCOPE: primary abstract and bibliographic record; open PDF exists via OpenReview but full methods not reread.**

- **Old account:** Under “good enough” processing, people may form a globally adequate parse but retain a competing inappropriate initial interpretation.
- **Pressure:** This cannot simply be assumed for every construction; what if the parser fails to build a coherent representation *at all*?
- **Changed object (DOCUMENTED in abstract):** Three Czech self-paced reading experiments included comprehension questions not only targeting initial misinterpretation but also analyses **never syntactically licensed during incremental processing**.
- **Reported result:** GP affects these additional questions too, supporting some unsuccessful global repair, not exclusively lingering of the old parse.
- **Idea genesis (RECONSTRUCTED):** Challenge the **scope of the response measure** rather than just measuring how long participants need: if old local meaning were the only lingering trace, questions about always-illicit structures should be preserved; finding damage outside that set changes the interpretation.
- **Scale:** A *new diagnostic of comprehension state* and evidence for a range of GP difficulty, **not** the first garden-path effect or general cognitive mechanism proof.
- **Limit:** This is Czech/human comprehension behavior; the abstract does not establish the detailed causal source of incompleteness. Full text/statistics pending.
- **Impact on our novelty:** 'LLMs might lack coherent complete meaning' is a mature **imported research question**; if our paper only asks more questions about a later region, it is not enough.
