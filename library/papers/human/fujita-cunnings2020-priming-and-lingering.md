# Fujita & Cunnings (2020) — Lingering misinterpretation in native and nonnative sentence processing: evidence from structural priming

*Applied Psycholinguistics*. [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/lingering-misinterpretation-in-native-and-nonnative-sentence-processing-evidence-from-structural-priming/773E27589D153A4A4F12B90FAE2DE45D). **Read scope: abstract, design and main results** (publisher page, 2026-10-12); supplements not read.

- **Design:** 36 item sets; prime (ambiguous vs comma-disambiguated) × target (ambiguous vs unambiguous) × lexical overlap (same vs different subordinate verb); eye tracking; L1 (n = 48) and L2 (n = 48) readers; comprehension question on the obsolete relation after targets.
- **Results:**
  - **Reading times:** priming only in total viewing time at the disambiguating region, and only with verb overlap. Ambiguous targets were read more slowly after ambiguous than after unambiguous primes.
  - **Comprehension accuracy:** lingering misinterpretation was present (11–17 points lower for ambiguous targets), but priming did not affect it.
  - No L1/L2 differences.
- **Relevance:** a human precedent for a **dissociation between priming and comprehension lingering** (by measure, not by item). Our E003 finds an item-level dissociation in LMs. This paper owns "priming and lingering accuracy dissociate in humans"; it does not own item-level LM evidence.
