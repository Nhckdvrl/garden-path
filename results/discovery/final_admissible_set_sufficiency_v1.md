# Selective evidence control: suppression, restoration, preservation

Date: 2026-09-27. **Working research framework, developed after G30.** This document changes neither historical registrations nor frozen results. Earlier working name: *final-admissible-set sufficiency*. Start with the [ConfB confirmation](../g24a/g24a_confb_analysis_v1.md), [ConfA post hoc reanalysis](../g24a/g24a_confa_final_set_reanalysis_v1.md), [G29/G29B assessment](../g29/g29_g29b_integrated_assessment.md), and [G30 factorial diagnostic](../g30/g30_factorial_diagnostic_v1.md).

## The natural question

**Can a model suppress disallowed evidence without changing the judgment warranted by the remaining information or damaging its use of still-valid evidence?**

Let `C` be the stable claim/task, `X` a displayed but disallowed evidence item, and `E` a later admissible item. Selective control asks for `F(C,E,X_excluded) ≈ F(C,E)`. This overall target separates into three capabilities. They require different comparisons and need not succeed together.

| Capability | Operational question | Minimum comparison |
|---|---|---|
| **Suppression (S)** | Does the *direct polarity effect* of disallowed `X` disappear? | Hold `C,E` fixed; change `X+` to `X−` under exclusion, and compare the resulting separation with the admitted-X separation. A near-zero signed mean over unrelated items is **not** sufficient. |
| **Restoration (R)** | After excluding `X`, is the same item's judgment close to the judgment without `X`? | Pair `F(C,E,X_excluded)` with `F(C,E)` item by item; report absolute deviation as well as signed bias. Include a frame-matched excluded-irrelevant comparison when attributing the cause to X's meaning. |
| **Preservation (P)** | Is the model's *response to valid E* retained after X is excluded? | Compare the effect of matched `E+` and `E−` (or reliability/strength levels) under clean and excluded-X histories. Report item-wise and model-wise interaction magnitudes, not just a pooled signed shift. |

For a graded readout, one preservation contrast is `L_X = F(C,E+,X_excluded) − F(C,E−,X_excluded)` versus `L_0 = F(C,E+) − F(C,E−)`, with `|L_X−L_0|` measuring changed valid-evidence sensitivity. This needs **paired E levels under each history**; a single final-score shift measures restoration/content influence, not preservation. Where a pre-E judgment is actually observed, compare within-history `Δ_E` instead. Neither definition assumes Bayes-optimal updating or a uniform direction of failure. Direct decisions and graded readouts should be reported separately.

The three capabilities are evaluation desiderata, not three established universal LLM failures. In particular, `S` can look strong while `R` is poor; whether `S` and `P` reliably decouple remains open. The wording “suppression ≠ restoration ≠ preservation” names distinct tests, not a theorem that every pair is statistically independent.

ConfB is the established paper core: **high suppression and large restoration error coexist on the same fresh material.** ConfA illustrates how a signed average can conceal item-wise restoration error. Its mean absolute ExcludePre shift is 19.50 versus 17.06 for AdmitPre, so a large absolute shift alone cannot identify an excluded-semantic mechanism. The [source audit](../../data/external/review/G24A_SOURCE_DATA_AUDIT_v1.md) passed and the [500-item review material](../../data/items/g24a_confa_review_v1.md) was frozen before inference; the new restoration estimand is still post hoc. G29B is the best natural-content clue about preservation, while G30 shows why a pooled signed effect can mislead.

## What is distinctive relative to nearby work

Generic susceptibility to irrelevant material is established. [Yang et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.674/) measure irrelevant-context effects on reasoning paths and accuracy; [Hwang et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.957/) test distracting instructions inside task input despite explicit instruction/input separation; [Llama See, Llama Do](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.791/) studies contextual entrainment and a no-distractor output comparison; [The Distracting Effect](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.892/) studies irrelevant RAG passages. [Context Length Alone Hurts](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1264/) shows that even additional tokens without semantic distraction can impair downstream evidence use. The headline **“excluded information can affect LLMs”** is therefore inadequate, and a P effect requires interpreting possible length/frame costs as well as semantic content.

The no-observation retraction comparator has [human prior](https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/93/1/476/8159675), while suppression versus deletion/restoration appears in [machine unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18505). Parameter unlearning also routinely balances forgetting and retain-set utility; [context-aware unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17620) examines whether forgotten knowledge remains usable when reintroduced in a prompt. Our proposed P is specifically **the marginal effect of a still-admissible E on the same item after an explicit in-context X exclusion**, not general retain-set accuracy or reintroduction of forgotten X. [From Evidence to Belief](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.531/) studies evidence reliability and informativeness, and [ReviseQA](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=Z4KBiAYXlI) studies adding/removing facts and rules. We should not claim to have invented counterfactual restoration, belief consistency, or Bayesian evidence evaluation.

Our candidate contribution is narrower and more testable: **under an explicit in-context eligibility rule, conventional apparent success at suppressing disallowed evidence can coexist with large paired restoration error; does achieving that suppression also preserve normal processing of what remains admissible?** This differs from ordinary distractor robustness because local exclusion compliance and collateral effects are measured together. It differs from generic final-set invariance by separating failure modes and asking whether they couple across items, models, and tasks. [Mirage of Model Editing](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.745/) and [Value–Action Gap](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.154/) are evaluation-story anchors, not identical settings.

## The thinnest and highest-value cell: preservation

The next test should put S, R, and P in **one natural, audited item family** rather than rerun D/N/I/R on a new domain. Each item needs a claim/decision context `C`; two semantically opposed but similarly plausible candidate X items; paired admissible E levels (`E+`/`E−`, or a meaningful strength ladder); a clean history; and matched initially-excluded histories. If an active-X arm is needed for the suppression denominator, use the **same X text** there. Keep final admissible evidence identical within each matched contrast. Direct decisions and graded confidence can both be collected, with confidence meaningful for the task rather than an arbitrary digit score.

Before inference, have the local free OpenCode model construct and audit **each item** against pinned natural sources; retain accepted and rejected candidates, relation judgments, source provenance, and item hashes. Human inspection can target audit exceptions and overall task validity. Freeze the sample and metrics before inference. Do not select only cases where the clean model uses E; report that subgroup separately if useful. Analyze each model before pooling. A genuine S/P decoupling requires **on the same items/model** both clear suppression of X polarity and sizable `L_X−L_0`; a mere N−I final-score shift is insufficient. R is the paired no-X deviation, with an excluded-irrelevant frame control for attribution. A decisive plot would show item/model S, R, and P estimates side by side rather than only aggregate means.

The outcome can change the story. If preservation is stable whenever suppression is strong, the paper remains a suppression–restoration evaluation result; G29B/G30 become boundary cases. If preservation also fails coherently while suppression succeeds, the stronger claim is that **selective removal can cause collateral distortion of valid-evidence processing**. A method would then have a natural target: improve R and P while retaining S and ordinary admitted-evidence use. No method or internal mechanism is presumed by current data.
