# G32 construct-identification pilot: what did exclusion actually measure?

**Status:** exploratory, 2026-09-28. The [registration](../discovery/g32_construct_identification_registration.md), [nearest-prior matrix](../discovery/g32_prior_estimand_matrix_v1.md), 56 frozen items, and independent material audit were committed before the full run. Three named local checkpoints completed 2016 cells each. Qwen and Gemma parsed 2016/2016; Mistral parsed 2015/2016. Its one missing value is a label typo (`PROBABABILITY: 100`, `g29_127`, open-world E+ D−), retained as raw text and treated as missing by the strict frozen parser. Mistral open-world claim-complete summaries use 55 items; other summaries use 56. All raw message bodies and hashes were checked against the frozen prompt compiler. One-item smoke files are separate and excluded.

## Main result: a query-dependent failure exists, but it is not a universal non-use failure

Mean 0–100 points across claims. `A_X` is mean itemwise `|A+−A−|` with X admissible, `D_X` is `|D+−D−|` under direct exclusion, `S_X` is the analogous hypothetical-self contrast. `Q` is the **signed** no-X query shift `F_S−F_D`. `D→B` and `S→B` are mean arm-wise absolute distances to the clean B response. `P_B/P_D/P_FS` are signed E+/E− leverage, where `P_D` averages D+ and D−.

Qwen's open-world `Q=+13.36` has claim-bootstrap 95% CI `[7.91,19.49]`; record-only `Q=+3.57` has CI `[-0.89,8.04]`, with a paired record-minus-open difference of `−9.79 [−16.00,−4.33]`. This is a **no-X-content** comparison: merely asking for a fresh-model prediction rather than a current judgment moves Qwen's answer. The effect is concentrated in refuting E: the open-world refuting-E `F_D→F_S` signed difference is `+24.93 [14.21,35.71]`, versus `+1.79` for supporting E. Fourteen of 56 refuting-E items flip from 0 in F_D to 100 in F_S; three of these 14 are among the 12 nonnumeric claims. In record-only, five refuting-E items show an upward shift of at least 50 points. This is not a pure numeric-threshold artifact, but the small prose subset cannot establish generality.

For Qwen open-world, self-prediction's absolute error against its actual clean response is 22.69, versus 5.96 for direct exclusion. Their **claim-paired difference** is `+16.73 [10.73,23.18]`. The hypothetical question also cuts mean permitted-E leverage within the redacted frame from 64.21 (F_D) to 41.07 (F_S). Thus a hypothetical-self restoration score can be poor even when the current direct answer is closer to B and retains much of E sensitivity. This does **not** prove a self-simulation module is defective: the query may change what the model takes “predict” to mean, and `Q` is not an additive causal share of an absolute error.

On the same materials, Gemma and Mistral have small signed no-X query shifts and much smaller S→B gaps. Mistral in particular shows active-X polarity leverage 47.81–54.42, direct-excluded leverage only 2.59–4.50, and clean-to-direct error around 4.6–4.8 while maintaining E leverage around 80–85. It is a counterexample to a *universal* failure of direct exclusion or clean-self prediction in this prospective task. It does not contradict ConfB's post-admission retraction result.

## What the controls rule in and out

The active-X calibration shows that X was not generally inert. The direct-excluded itemwise X effect is smaller than active X by paired `16.46 [8.37,24.78]` points for Qwen open-world, `14.12 [7.68,21.01]` for Gemma open-world and `43.31 [35.43,51.30]` for Mistral open-world. Still, Qwen direct-excluded itemwise X effects of 9.51 (open-world) and 11.87 (record-only) are not zero. A small signed D+−D− mean (3.97 and 1.17) would again exaggerate suppression. Gemma and Mistral also have nonzero absolute effects. The claim is **partial selective non-use**, strongest for Mistral on this substrate, not perfect causal irrelevance.

The permitted-E check also matters. All three models retain large mean E leverage in direct exclusion. Yet Qwen's mean *absolute itemwise* change in E leverage relative to its redacted direct frame is 11.83 open-world and 17.24 record-only, despite a small signed mean difference. Therefore “P_D≈P_B” in aggregate does not prove fully preserved use of E at the claim level. Conversely, no model collapsed to a constant 50 as a general exclusion strategy. Most outputs are near 0 or 100, creating floor/ceiling effects; the active and E controls mitigate but do not erase that constraint.

Record-only does **not** generally repair direct X leakage: Qwen's itemwise direct X effect rises from 9.51 to 11.87 (paired CI for record-minus-open `[-2.68,7.66]`), Gemma's falls from 7.00 to 3.25 (CI `[-8.64,0.99]`), and Mistral remains low. The clearer Qwen contract interaction concerns the **self-prediction question**, not a broad rule that open-world judgment cannot exclude X. A “record-only solves exclusion” headline would be false.

## Relation to ConfB and novelty ceiling

This pilot identifies a real behavioral ambiguity in calling every clean-context discrepancy a selective non-use failure. It is more specific than the already-known Self-Blinding suppression/restoration gap: G32 independently varies legal E and excluded X, includes a matched redacted frame for both direct and hypothetical questions, and calibrates whether X matters when active. The new inference is that, at least in Qwen's open-world task, the **no-content hypothetical query itself** can move answers against actual clean responses and attenuate the use of refuting admissible E.

That inference is **model- and task-bounded**. G32 uses X excluded from its first display. ConfB's 200 fresh cases use X first encountered as evidence and then retrospectively withdrawn under a counterfactual-delete rule. Their outputs, material family and readout differ. In ConfB the same three models had mean clean-counterfactual center errors of 33.26 (Qwen), 23.65 (Gemma), and 27.35 (Mistral); G32's much smaller Mistral S→B error of 5–7 cannot by itself explain the ConfB gap away. Nor should the G32 no-X frame contrast be called a *causal decomposition* of ConfB's MAE. The G32 examples overlap prior G29/G31 work (41/56 with G31), X notes are constructed, and 44/56 claims are numeric/threshold-like. One model's 14 stark flips are scientifically interesting, but selling “hypothetical prompts change answers” as a paper-level idea would repeat G18's triviality mistake.

**Current ceiling:** valuable construct diagnostic and research-direction selector; not yet a general Main-paper behavioral law. The stable ConfB phenomenon remains, but its higher-level interpretation is not settled. G32 argues against immediately moving to hidden-state probes or a restoration LoRA. It also argues against declaring the entire project a Self-Blinding replication: evidence eligibility, legal-E use and *when* X gained standing remain distinct objects to identify.

## Most informative next move

The next causal boundary is **eligibility history**, using the same 56 crossed E/X items and readout. Keep the G32 direct-versus-self-prediction and redacted-frame comparisons, but replace “X excluded from first display” with “X first admitted, then explicitly withdrawn before the final judgment”; hold final admissible E and answer format fixed. This is a small bridge to ConfB, not a new benchmark. It asks whether the large gap is a property of retrospective withdrawal, a property of the hypothetical query, or their interaction. Register the exact chronology and matched redacted *withdrawal-event* controls before outputs. Inspect Mistral first conceptually: it is strong and stable under G32 prospective exclusion yet had substantial ConfB post-admission restoration error, so the post-admission manipulation is maximally discriminating.

If Mistral and Gemma acquire large direct or hypothetical errors only after prior admission while active-X and E leverage remain interpretable, the research object becomes **eligibility transition / status reversal**, worth fresh natural confirmation and then perhaps a mechanism. If the new history does not change them, prioritize material/readout/operator differences between G32 and ConfB, and do not attribute ConfB to “retrospective withdrawal” without evidence. If Qwen's no-X hypothetical shift persists across ordinary wording paraphrases and a second less numerical domain while direct E use remains comparatively intact, a construct-validity/evaluation story becomes plausible. If it disappears with one paraphrase, treat it as prompt-specific, not a new ability distinction. These are outcome-robust branches; none presupposes a method or a particular effect direction.

## 2026-09-30 interpretation correction

The [project reassessment](../audits/project_reassessment_2026_09_30.md) withdraws the automatic priority assigned to the eligibility-history experiment above. ConfB does not actually present an initial admission rule or obtain an earlier decision from the model: its compiler renders claim, evidence and CF ruling in one prompt. ConfB/G32 differences also change material, valid E, query and readout. G29 already constrains a revoked-versus-never-admissible history account. A chronology bridge could resolve a bounded attribution question, but its scientific ceiling is not established by the cross-study difference. Self-Blinding already compares direct ignore and counterfactual prediction and reports larger blinded-baseline error for the latter; the distinction alone is not G32 novelty. Finally, the record-only active-X calibration has a scope ambiguity: the contract restricts use to the header-marked admissible record E, while the A-cell status permits a separate X note alongside E. Its active-X denominator should not be treated as an unambiguous contract calibration. Raw data, frozen prompts, registrations and computed numbers remain unchanged.
