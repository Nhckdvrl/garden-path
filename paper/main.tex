\title{Language Models Cannot Unread a Garden Path:\\ Abandoned Analyses Persist While Errors Drive Adaptation}

\author{Anonymous}

\begin{abstract}
After reading \ex{While the man hunted the deer ran into the woods}, a reader must abandon the analysis in which \ex{the deer} is the object of \ex{hunted}. In humans, such abandoned analyses still prime later production \citep{vangompel2006}. This is a rare case where the two main theories of structural priming disagree. Residual activation predicts priming of the \emph{abandoned} analysis. Error-driven learning predicts adaptation toward the \emph{resolved} one, because the prediction error falls on the resolved structure. We use this case to ask how language models (LMs) carry an interpretation forward after giving it up. Across \TODO{N} open LMs (0.1B--70B), 241 garden-path item families and free generation, we find both forces at once:
\begin{itemize}
\item a residue of the abandoned object analysis that biases later production;
\item an error-driven adaptation toward the resolved parse.
\end{itemize}
Each force responds to its own causal manipulation, and the measure determines which one dominates. Activation patching locates the residue in the noun phrase's pre-disambiguation states. A causal LM cannot recompute these states, so they persist in context as the object reading. We also show that the human paradigm's raw statistic reverses sign in LMs because models copy the prime's punctuation, and that question-answering ``lingering misinterpretation'' is a separate phenomenon.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Garden-path sentences force a reader to build an analysis and then abandon it. In \ex{While the man hunted the deer ran into the woods}, \ex{the deer} is first taken as the object of \ex{hunted}. The verb \ex{ran} shows that it is the subject of the main clause. How much of the abandoned analysis survives reanalysis is a long-standing question in psycholinguistics \citep{christianson2001,sturt2007,slattery2013}. It has recently been asked of LMs too, mostly through comprehension questions \citep{li2024incremental,amouyal2025,amouyal2026} and surprisal at the disambiguating word \citep{huang2024,yoshida2026}.

We study a different trace of the abandoned analysis: its effect on what comes \emph{next}. \citet{vangompel2006} showed that after reading a garden-path sentence, people complete a new fragment (\ex{While the nurse washed}) with a transitive structure more often than after a comma-disambiguated control. The ruled-out object analysis primes later production. The case matters for theory as well as for the specific phenomenon, for the following reason. The two dominant accounts of structural priming are residual activation \citep{pickering1998} and error-driven implicit learning \citep{chang2006,jaeger2013}. For ordinary primes they predict the same direction, which is why decades of work, including all LM priming studies \citep{sinclair2022,jumelet2024,zhou2025icl,pucci2026}, cannot tell them apart. In a garden path they disagree. Residual activation predicts priming of the abandoned transitive analysis. Error-driven learning predicts adaptation toward the resolved intransitive-plus-new-clause structure, because that is where the prediction error falls. Humans show only the net effect.

LMs let us measure both forces separately. We score next-word probabilities and free generations after garden-path primes, with controls for punctuation, verb bias, case marking and local coherence. We make four contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The abandoned analysis persists.} In every model we test, a garden-path prime raises later object attachment in production, both in log-probabilities and in sampled completions. This holds on held-out items, with case instead of punctuation as the disambiguator, and only when an object analysis was actually licensed (\S\ref{sec:residue}).
\item \textbf{A second, error-driven force pushes the other way.} The same prime lowers the surprise of later garden-path resolutions, in proportion to its own disambiguation error. We manipulate the error and the initial commitment within item families, and each manipulation moves its own force. Which force dominates depends on whether the measure is production-like or comprehension-like (\S\ref{sec:twoforces}).
\item \textbf{The residue lives in stale states.} A causal transformer computes the representation of \ex{the deer} before it sees \ex{ran} and never recomputes it. Replacing these states with states computed under the subject analysis, leaving everything else unchanged, \TODO{removes most of} the residue. Patching the post-disambiguation states instead \TODO{does not}; those states carry the adaptation (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).
\item \textbf{Methodological consequences.} Transferred naively, the human statistic reverses sign in several model families: models copy the prime's punctuation and write garden-path sentences themselves. QA-based ``lingering misinterpretation'' is item-by-item unrelated to the production residue (\S\ref{sec:method-lessons}).
\end{enumerate}

These results speak to three communities. For psycholinguistics, they give a working system in which both classical accounts of priming operate at once and in opposite directions, and they yield a testable prediction for humans: more surprising disambiguations should produce \emph{less} abandoned-analysis priming. For LM science, they show that ``in-context learning is error-driven learning'' \citep{zhou2025icl} is incomplete: next to error-driven adaptation, LMs carry forward representations built under interpretations they have since abandoned, for an architectural reason. For evaluation, they show that a human paradigm can give the opposite answer when its statistic is transferred without the controls the model's own behaviour requires.

\section{Background}

\paragraph{Two accounts of structural priming.} Speakers and readers reuse structures they have just processed. On the \emph{residual-activation} account, processing a structure leaves its representation (for example, a combinatorial node linked to the verb) temporarily active, so it is easier to retrieve again \citep{pickering1998}. On the \emph{error-driven} account, processing a prime is a small learning episode. Expectations shift toward the structure that was actually encountered, in proportion to how unexpected it was \citep{chang2006}. This predicts larger priming from less expected primes, the inverse-frequency effect, which is found in humans \citep{jaeger2013} and in LMs \citep{jumelet2024,zhou2025icl}. For an ordinary prime both accounts predict priming of the prime's structure. The inverse-frequency effect separates them only indirectly.

\paragraph{Garden-path primes.} A temporarily ambiguous prime is a direct test. \citet{vangompel2006} found more transitive completions after \ex{While the man hunted the deer ran\ldots} than after its comma version (33.6\% vs.\ 29.1\% in their Exp.~1). They read this as residual activation of the abandoned analysis. The error-driven account makes the opposite prediction. The surprise in a garden path is the discovery that \ex{the deer} begins a new clause, so learning should strengthen the resolved, intransitive analysis. This matches syntactic adaptation in comprehension: garden-path difficulty decreases with exposure \citep{fine2013}. Human data report only the net of the two forces. \citet{fujita2020} found that priming of a garden-path structure changes reading times but not comprehension accuracy.

\paragraph{Garden paths in LMs.} LM surprisal at the disambiguating word predicts that garden-path effects exist but underestimates their size \citep{huang2024}; see \citet{yoshida2026} for trained models that close the gap. In comprehension, LMs answer \ex{Did the man hunt the deer?} with Yes much as humans do \citep{li2024incremental,amouyal2025}. \citet{hanna2025} found features for both readings in small models and showed that the QA circuit hardly reuses them. LM structural priming has been studied with unambiguous primes only \citep{sinclair2022,michaelov2023,jumelet2024,zhou2025icl,pucci2026}. We know of no study of what an abandoned analysis does to later processing in LMs.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Materials.} NP/Z garden-path families from \citet{amouyal2025} (69) and the SAP benchmark \citep{huang2024} (24) form the discovery set. Held-out families come from \citet{cehakova2025} and SyntaxGym (48). Further families come from the appendix of \citet{christianson2001} (54). Each family $f$ provides primes in four conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item GP: \ex{While the man hunted the deer ran\ldots};
\item CUE: \ex{\ldots hunted, the deer ran\ldots};
\item NB and NC: the same sentence with a strictly intransitive verb (\ex{slept}), without and with the comma;
\end{itemize}
plus a full transitive prime (TB: \ex{\ldots hunted the deer it ran\ldots}).

\paragraph{Targets and measures.} Each prime is followed by targets from every other family that shares no content word with it. The production-like measure $L$ is the log-probability preference for attaching the target NP as an object: $L=\log P(\ex{While the nurse washed the baby,})-\log P(\ex{While the nurse washed, the baby})$. The comprehension-like measure $S$ is the surprisal of the disambiguating word in a garden-path target. In free production, the model continues \ex{While the nurse washed} by pure sampling, and the continuation is coded as transitive or intransitive by a part-of-speech rule system (99.7\% accuracy on 1,066 gold sentences).

\paragraph{The residue contrast.} Comparing GP with CUE confounds the analysis with punctuation, and comparing GP with NB confounds it with the verb. We use
\begin{equation}
A=(\mathrm{GP}-\mathrm{NB})-(\mathrm{CUE}-\mathrm{NC}),
\end{equation}
which is matched for punctuation within pairs and for verb across pairs. Inference uses a two-way bootstrap over prime and target families. Cross-model summaries use random-effects meta-analysis. Every confirmatory experiment had its predictions frozen in a dated card before data collection; exploratory steps are labelled as such.

\section{The abandoned analysis persists}\label{sec:residue}

\paragraph{Log-probability production.} On the production-like measure $L$, a garden-path prime raises the later preference for object attachment more than the controls do. In the discovery set the residue $A_L$ is positive in 17/17 models, with a pooled estimate of $+0.70$ nats [0.53, 0.86] (random-effects meta-analysis). That is 86\% of the effect of a prime in which the NP really is the object ($r=A/A_{\text{full}}$). The residue is positive in every model on the held-out NP/Z families (17/17) and on the Christianson items (16/16). \TODO{update with the scaled panel.}

\paragraph{It is not punctuation.} Comma omission is a salient property of garden-path primes, and models adapt to it: NB primes without a comma change later comma use in 17/17 models. We therefore replaced the comma with case (E009). In \ex{While the man hunted they ran\ldots} the nominative \ex{they} rules out the object analysis, and the verb and the missing comma stay identical within the pair. The item-level relation between object expectation at the verb and the residue stays positive in 17/17 models.

\paragraph{It needs a licensed analysis.} The string \ex{hunted the deer} might prime by local coherence alone \citep{tabor2004}. A passive prime (\ex{While the man was hunted the deer ran\ldots}) contains the same string but never licenses an object reading. Its residue is a median 10\% of the garden-path residue, and it is significantly negative in 7/17 models (E011). The residue therefore reflects an analysis that the model actually entertained.

\paragraph{It reaches free generation.} \TODO{scaled panel.} We sampled two continuations of \ex{While the nurse washed} for every prime--target pair (pure sampling, 57k prompts per model). With the same contrast, garden-path primes raise transitive completions in 8/8 models. The pooled $A_{gen}$ is $+0.056$ [0.039, 0.072], 21\% of the effect of a true transitive prime. Across models, $A_{gen}$ correlates with $A_L$ ($\rho=.81$).

\section{Two forces with opposite signs}\label{sec:twoforces}

The residue is not the only thing a garden-path prime leaves behind. On the comprehension-like measure $S$, the net contrast $A_S$ is close to zero on average (pooled $+0.05$ [$-0.03$, 0.13]) and varies in sign across models. The two largest Qwen models show significantly negative values, meaning \emph{less} expectation of a transitive continuation after a garden-path prime. Two opposite forces explain this pattern.

\paragraph{Item level.} Across families, $A$ rises with the model's object expectation at the ambiguous verb. This is the \emph{commitment} slope, positive in 20/20 models in the punctuation-free E009 design. $A$ falls with the prime's own disambiguation surprisal. This is the \emph{error} slope, negative in 18/20 models. The two predictors are nearly uncorrelated, and they correspond to the residual-activation and error-driven accounts.

\paragraph{The error force is causal.} Holding the prime prefix fixed, we varied only the continuation after the NP, and with it the surprisal at the disambiguation point (E006). More surprising disambiguations produce less residue within a family ($t$ from $-4.1$ to $-6.4$ in the three models with a clear discovery-set error slope; $\approx 0$ in the two models where discovery predicted $\approx 0$). Surprisal summed over the whole continuation predicts little. The relevant error is the one at the disambiguation point.

\paragraph{The commitment force is causal.} E013 (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) intervenes on the commitment directly: removing the object-analysis states of the NP removes the residue. A behavioural manipulation (E014) gave weaker evidence. Preceding contexts that made the NP a likely object strengthened the garden path by 0.9--1.7 nats in all ten models, and they \emph{raised} $A_S$ in 9/10 (7 significant). An error-only account predicts a decrease, because a stronger garden path also means a larger disambiguation error. The pre-registered manipulation check on verb-level transitivity failed, so we report E014 as supporting rather than confirmatory evidence.

\paragraph{The measure decides which force dominates.} The residue dominates the production-like measure. $A_L$ is positive in every model on three independent item sets, and its error slope is weak. Adaptation dominates the comprehension-like measure. In a prospective test on the Christianson items (E010), the error slope was more negative on $S$ than on $L$ in 14/16 models. In human terms, van Gompel's production paradigm sees the residue, and adaptation studies in comprehension \citep{fine2013} see the error-driven force. LMs show both, from the same prime, depending on the readout.

\paragraph{Boundary.} With the same frozen rule, neither force appears for NP/S garden paths (\ex{The man knew the answer was wrong}). There the reanalysis is monotonic: the NP is reattached, not stripped of its role. The error force does appear for main-verb/reduced-relative garden paths (6/9 models), which, like NP/Z, are non-monotonic. The two-force structure therefore belongs to reanalyses that destroy a committed structure.

\section{The residue lives in stale states}\label{sec:mechanism}

Why should an abandoned analysis persist in an LM at all? A causal transformer computes the states at \ex{the deer} before it reaches \ex{ran}, and it never revisits them. In \ex{While the man hunted the deer ran\ldots} these states are exactly the states of the transitive prime \ex{While the man hunted the deer it ran\ldots}, because the two share the prefix. Everything later in the context, including the next sentence, attends to keys and values built under the object analysis. Call this the \emph{stale-state} hypothesis. The alternative is that the residue is carried by the main-clause states (\ex{ran into the woods.}), which processed the disambiguation and could encode the reanalysis episode.

\paragraph{Design.} We construct a counterfactual prime in which the NP was analysed as a subject from the start.
\begin{itemize}
\item The residual stream at the NP tokens is overwritten, at every layer, with the states of the same tokens in the comma version (\ex{While the man hunted, the deer}).
\item The rest of the prime is recomputed (run A); the unpatched prime is run B.
\item The target then attends to a hybrid cache. It takes run-A keys and values on one span (NP, main clause MC, or both) and run-B keys and values elsewhere.
\item The same procedure is applied to the NB prime with the NC prime as donor. This control removes anything the patch does merely by importing ``after a comma'' information.
\end{itemize}
We report the retained fraction $\rho_s=A_s/A$ on $L$, and the change $\Delta_s=A_s-A$ on $S$. Predictions were frozen before the runs.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:mech} shows the results for eight models.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The NP states carry the production residue.} Replacing only the NP's states removes most of the residue: $\rho_{NP}$ ranges from 0.01 (Qwen2.5-32B) to 0.71. Replacing the main-clause states removes little or nothing ($\rho_{MC}$ 0.74--1.94). $\rho_{NP}<\rho_{MC}$ in 8/8 models; the frozen magnitude criterion $\rho_{NP}\le .5$ holds in 6/8.
\item \textbf{The two forces sit in different spans.} On the comprehension-like measure, the two spans push in opposite directions in every model. Removing the stale NP states lowers $A_S$ ($\Delta_{NP}<0$, 8/8): they carry the residue. Removing the garden-pathed main-clause states raises $A_S$ by 0.2--2.0 nats ($\Delta_{MC}>0$, 8/8): they carry the error-driven adaptation.
\end{itemize}
The two forces of \S\ref{sec:twoforces} are therefore not an analysis construct. They are read from different positions of the same prime. In the larger models, removing the main-clause states even \emph{raises} the production residue ($\rho_{MC}=1.34$ in Qwen2.5-7B, 1.94 in Qwen2.5-32B). The observed net residue thus understates the persistence of the abandoned analysis wherever the error-driven force is strong.

\section{Consequences for transferring human paradigms}\label{sec:method-lessons}

\paragraph{The human statistic reverses.} \citet{vangompel2006} compared transitive completions after GP and comma primes. In LMs this raw contrast is positive in only 4/11 models, and it is significantly \emph{negative} in 6 of the 7 Llama and Qwen models. The reason is visible in the completions. After a comma-less prime, models reproduce its style: up to 28\% of completions after a GP prime are themselves comma-less garden-path sentences, against 0.4--2.4\% after a comma prime (11/11 models). Human participants completed fragments orally, so punctuation could not align. A naive transfer would conclude that LMs show the \emph{opposite} of the human effect. Matching punctuation and verb (our $A$) recovers the residue in 10/11 models.

\paragraph{QA lingering is a different phenomenon.} LMs answer \ex{Did the man hunt the deer?} with Yes after the garden path, often and more so for longer ambiguous regions, as humans do \citep{christianson2001}. But item by item, this comprehension ``lingering'' is unrelated to the production residue: $r$ from $-.30$ to $+.15$ in 10/10 models that pass the comprehension controls, with split-half reliabilities of .77--.98 for both measures. It also fails to track the garden path itself. A context sentence that restates the main clause reduces it in 7/7 models. A full reordered paraphrase, which removes the garden path more thoroughly, reduces it less. Reports of LM ``lingering'' based on QA \citep{li2024incremental,amouyal2025} and the representational persistence we measure here are therefore not the same thing. A claim that LMs are human-like or unlike humans on garden paths has to say which of the two it measures.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What garden paths reveal about priming.} The residual-activation and error-driven accounts of priming have mostly been treated as rivals, and they make the same prediction for ordinary primes. Garden-path primes separate them. In LMs both forces are present in the same context window, with opposite signs. Each responds to its own manipulation, and each is read from a different part of the prime. The residue lives in the abandoned constituent's states. The adaptation lives in the states that processed the disambiguation. A human prediction follows from this two-force picture. If people show the same superposition, then garden paths whose disambiguation is more surprising should yield \emph{less} abandoned-analysis priming in production, and more adaptation in comprehension. The van Gompel paradigm combined with cloze-normed disambiguation strength can test this directly.

\paragraph{Why an LM cannot unread.} In humans, ``residual activation'' is a metaphor for a memory trace whose substrate is unknown. In a causal transformer the substrate is concrete. Representations are written once, at the time each token is read, and later tokens consult them through attention without revising them. A reanalysis can change what later positions compute, but it cannot change what the earlier positions hold. Our patching results show that this architectural fact has behavioural consequences: a later sentence reads the abandoned analysis from those positions. The same logic applies to any interpretation an LM builds and later rejects within its context. Examples include a misread instruction, a retracted claim in a dialogue, or an abandoned hypothesis in a reasoning trace. The garden path is the cleanest case in which the abandoned interpretation, its strength and its rejection can all be measured.

\paragraph{In-context learning is not only error-driven.} \citet{zhou2025icl} showed an inverse-frequency effect in LM priming and concluded that in-context learning is a form of error-driven learning. Our error force, causal within families and located in the post-disambiguation states, supports that conclusion. But an error-driven learner would not prime the analysis that was \emph{rejected}. The residue shows that context effects in LMs also include persistence of representations that the error signal has already overruled.

\section*{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item We study English NP/Z garden paths in depth. MV/RR and NP/S serve as boundary cases only, and we test neither other languages nor other ambiguity types.
\item The models are open-weight models up to 70B, scored with exact log-probabilities. Closed models cannot be patched and were not tested.
\item The free-production coding is rule-based (99.7\% on gold material sentences). The audit on real generations was done by a single annotator, not by independent human annotators.
\item The behavioural commitment manipulation (E014) failed its pre-registered manipulation check. Our causal evidence for the residue force therefore rests mainly on activation patching.
\item The two-force account and the contrast $A$ were first formulated after inspecting two models in the discovery set. Confirmation comes from held-out items, prospective tests and frozen predictions; held-out tests that failed are reported in the appendix.
\item We make a prediction for humans but run no human experiment.
\end{itemize}
