## 1. What changed since V6

V6 correctly revoked V5's premature promotion of the zero/nonzero anomaly.
The new audit makes three further changes.

1. **Zero/nonzero is no longer treated as a live paper-identity lead.** It is kept
   as an empirical anomaly / historical asset, but the combination of cross-model
   instability, task-family concentration, sparse independent skeletons, and
   conceptual overlap is too severe to justify spending another pilot on a
   "zero law."
2. **The BTF3 G12→G15 chain is promoted only as a scientific/mechanistic asset,
   not as a paper mainline.** It is stronger than V6 made explicit, but its
   behavioral heterogeneity and 2026 neighboring literature make generic
   "shared decision state" or "irrelevant outcome contamination" claims unsafe.
3. **The simple-data search has now killed several attractive-sounding
   distinctions before generation.** The current best surviving *search shape*
   concerns whether models condition the evidential value of an observation on
   the process that generated / selected / could have revealed it. This is
   **not yet authorized for a pilot**.

The discipline remains:

```
simple question
→ conceptual novelty assassination
→ scale/growth-path test
→ only then a tiny discovery pilot
```

not:

```
interesting-sounding contrast
→ run models
→ invent a story around whatever happens
```

# 2. Old assets: revised verdicts

## 2.1 Zero / nonzero — KILL AS PAPER IDENTITY

The old Stage-3 materials contain a real aggregate 0-vs-1% order interaction in
several models, but they do not establish a stable qualitative zero boundary.

Reasons:

- the formal zero-specific interaction is positive/significant in only 2/6
  models, significantly reversed in Mistral-Small-24B, and unresolved in 3/6;
- the 144 nominal items collapse to roughly 32 independent semantic skeletons,
  with some of the largest effects concentrated in families with only five base
  templates;
- family-wise effects change sharply by model;
- one outcome family already contains an information-boundary instruction and
  is not a clean domain replicate;
- the phenomenon is not a pure REI-ratio artifact, but removing that metric
  problem does not repair the generality problem;
- "zero is special," nonlinear evidence weighting, selective forgetting and
  context exclusion all have direct neighboring literatures.

**Decision:** archive as an empirical anomaly and possible supporting diagnostic.
Do not construct a fresh zero dataset merely to chase replication. A future,
independently motivated paper may reuse the observation if its new RQ predicts it.

---

## 2.2 Explicit-outcome paradox — HIGH-RISK ANOMALY, NO PILOT YET

The observation remains genuine and counterintuitive on the frozen materials:

> removing the explicit YES/NO outcome sentence increases the future packet's
> influence on the reconstructed earlier judgment.

It is not explained by the known redaction leak: the contrast becomes larger on
the leak-free subset, and the redacted packet retains large licensed evidential
leverage.

However, the current manipulation changes several things simultaneously:
conclusion-shaped language, length, packet→task distance, and how strongly the
block advertises itself as a resolution artifact. Human hindsight literature
already shows that the *form/type* of outcome information modulates hindsight,
and recent LLM work on epistemic vigilance / linguistic encoding makes a generic
"format changes acceptance" story unsafe.

**Decision:** keep as HIGH-RISK anomaly. No clean matched-sentence replication is
authorized until the conceptual contribution can be stated in a way that is not
"outcome format changes hindsight" or "salient labels are easier to discount."

A potentially interesting sentence remains:

> explicit conclusions can be easier to quarantine than equally diagnostic
> distributed evidence.

But this is a hypothesis-shaped observation, not a contribution yet.

---

## 2.3 BTF3 G12→G15 — MECHANISTIC ASSET, NOT CURRENT PAPER IDENTITY

V6 under-emphasized this chain.

### Behavioral fact (G12)

Holding the recipient question and all non-packet text fixed, replacing a
verdict-redacted *unrelated* future packet that supports NO with one that
supports YES shifts the current judgment in the YES direction:

- Qwen3.5-9B: +4.41 pp [1.27, 7.55] — preregistered indeterminate;
- Gemma-3-12B-IT: +17.50 pp [14.76, 20.33] — causal-entrainment gate passed;
- Mistral-Small-24B: +1.55 pp [0.55, 2.55] — preregistered practical null.

The panel verdict was therefore correctly **indeterminate**. Directional
consistency is descriptive; magnitude heterogeneity is load-bearing.

### Mechanistic fact (G13→G15, Gemma)

- packet-local donor-outcome information is decodable but its scalar swap is not
  the causal bottleneck (G13);
- the influence becomes causally transferable late at the answer position (G14);
- a fresh donor assignment prospectively confirms a recipient-conditioned late
  decision coordinate whose bidirectional interchange transfers 6–9 pp in the
  late network, with specificity controls (G15).

This is a serious mechanistic result.

### Why it still does not become the paper

2025–2026 work already covers several abstractions that a reviewer could use to
compress it:

- irrelevant-context / distractor interference;
- anchoring by unrelated prior judgments or scores;
- low-dimensional/shared decode-time decision subspaces;
- causal answer/evidence-aligned latent states that dissociate from final output;
- cross-task activation steering.

Therefore neither of these headlines is safe:

> "irrelevant information biases decisions"

> "LLMs have a shared decision direction"

The only potentially distinctive object is narrower:

> unrelated outcome-direction evidence is transformed into a
> recipient-conditioned causal decision state.

That claim is currently strongest in one model, while the behavioral effect is
small-to-null in another.

**Decision:** retain G12→G15 as a high-value mechanistic asset and possible
support for a future RQ. Do not promote it to a mainline by itself.

## 2.4 G0 / G18 / G22

- **G0:** strong empirical asset, conceptual paper identity still high-risk.

# 4. What the ACL-2026 award papers change about our search policy

The right lesson is **not** "the cognitive/linguistic distinction itself must be
new to science."

The ACL-2026 Best Paper on the imperfective paradox starts from a classic
semantic phenomenon. Its paper-level novelty is an LLM-specific empirical law:
a **Teleological Bias** that systematically pushes goal-directed events toward
completion, can override explicit cancellation, and connects behavior,
representation, and intervention.

That is the correct template:

```
classic/simple substrate
+
new, memorable LLM-specific law
+
breadth
+
deep causal/explanatory descent
```

Therefore a new candidate may use an old human reasoning distinction, but it
must discover something stronger than:

> "LLMs are imperfect at distinction X."

# 5. Current surviving search shape — how was the evidence generated?

## Status: PRE-PILOT NOVELTY SEARCH ONLY

Working research object:

> **Do language models condition the evidential value of the same observation on
> the process that generated, selected, or could have revealed that observation?**

This is broader than the slogan "absence of evidence is not evidence of
absence." The scientific object is the **observation process / sampling
mechanism**.

Human Bayesian/cognitive work establishes that the same observed sample can
rationally support different inferences depending on how it was sampled or
censored. The current LLM Bayesian literature establishes broad failures of
Bayesian updating, but the present search has not yet found a systematic LLM
study whose object is sensitivity to the *sampling/observation mechanism* itself.

That gap is promising but not yet sufficient.

---

## 5.1 Research Question

Can an LLM distinguish **what was observed** from **how likely that observation
would have been under each hypothesis**, especially when the observation is a
non-event?

In one line:

> **Does the model reason about the evidence-generating process, or only about
> the evidence content?**

---

## 5.2 One-example test

Two teams search identical forests for a fox and both report:

> "We did not see a fox."

Team A used a camera system that would detect a present fox 99% of the time.
Team B made a brief visual check that would detect one only 10% of the time.

The first non-detection is much stronger evidence that there is no fox.

A reader understands the object immediately; no invented ontology is needed.

---

## 5.3 Minimal dataset if a pilot is eventually authorized

No pilot is authorized now, but the design budget is deliberately shallow.

Possible 2×2 core:

1. **high observability + non-detection**
2. **low observability + non-detection**
3. **high observability + detection** (utility/understanding anchor)
4. **low observability + detection** (utility/understanding anchor)

Alternative minimal-pair substrate: identical positive examples under
**random sampling** vs **deliberate positive-example sampling**, using a tiny
explicit hypothesis space.

Each item should fit in 2–4 sentences. No external corpus, no LLM judge, no
world ontology.

---

## 5.4 Gold

Use an explicit prior plus observation-process sensitivity/specificity and
compute the Bayesian posterior exactly, or use pairwise monotonic comparisons
whose ordering is mathematically fixed.

The model should never be judged by another LLM.

A separate recognition query can ask the directly observable intermediate:

> "If a fox were present, which search would be more likely to find it?"

This permits a clean potential dissociation:

```
correct model of observability
≠
correct evidential update
```

without inventing a latent state.

---

## 5.5 Novelty threat map

This candidate is **not safe**. The strongest threats are:

1. **Bayesian teaching enables probabilistic reasoning in LLMs** (Nature
   Communications 2026): broad normative Bayesian updating failures and a
   transferable intervention.
2. **BayesBench: Evaluating LLM Belief Trajectories Under Multi-Turn Evidence
   Accumulation** (2026): exact Bayesian belief trajectories; importantly,
   identifies a latent-inference → downstream-use gap.
3. **Are LLM Belief Updates Consistent with Bayes' Theorem?** (2025): broad
   Bayesian coherence across generated scenarios.
4. **QUITE** (EMNLP 2024): natural-language Bayesian reasoning with explicit
   probabilistic relationships.
5. Clinical work on **pre/post-test probability after negative diagnostic
   results** already demonstrates that LLMs can perform some negative-test
   Bayesian updates.
6. **Competing Biases underlie Overconfidence and Underconfidence in LLMs**
   (Nature Machine Intelligence 2026): systematic asymmetric evidence
   weighting, including overweighting contradictory advice.
7. Human/cognitive work on **absence of evidence**, selective sampling,
   sampling assumptions, and censoring supplies the normative theory but also
   means the underlying cognitive distinction is not new.

### Reviewer kill sentence we must defeat

> "This is just another tiny instance of poor Bayesian updating."

If the only finding is quantitative posterior error, **KILL**.

---

## 5.6 What result could actually be novel?

A paper-worthy discovery would need a simple, stable law such as one of these
*shapes* (not predictions we are entitled to assume):

- **observation-process blindness:** models accurately state detectability /
  sampling mechanism but their downstream update is nearly invariant to it;
- **negative-evidence asymmetry:** process sensitivity appears for observed
  events but collapses specifically for non-events, despite matched normative
  likelihood ratios;
- **selection-content dissociation:** models track sample contents but fail to
  condition on why those contents entered the sample, with a robust
  cross-domain signature;
- an unexpected scaling or intervention law that is specific to generative
  process reasoning rather than generic arithmetic/Bayes performance.

Without such a law, there is no paper.

---

## 5.7 Growth path if — and only if — discovery is strong

```
C1  Simple behavioral phenomenon:
    same observation receives the wrong/invariant evidential weight when only
    its observation process changes.

↓
C2  Stronger dissociation/law:
    models explicitly recover the observation/sampling mechanism but fail to
    use it; or a process-specific asymmetry generalizes across search,
    diagnosis, sampling, and reporting.

↓
C3  Mechanism/intervention:
    identify where observation-process information and hypothesis content are
    represented, test whether they are composed at the decision site, and use
    a causal intervention or explicit factorization that selectively restores
    the missing dependence.
```

The mechanism phase would reuse the repository's strongest existing skill:
controlled contrasts + causal representation analysis. It should not begin
until C1/C2 exist behaviorally.

# 5B. Secondary independent search shape — partition / unpacking dependence

## Status: SECONDARY SEARCH ONLY / lower priority

A separate search considered whether LLM probability judgments change when the same
event space is merely packed or unpacked into more explicitly named alternatives.

This passes the simple-data test, but its theoretical substrate is the classic
**Support Theory** literature (Tversky & Koehler; Rottenstreich & Tversky), where
unpacking and partition dependence are already canonical human probability-judgment
effects.

The current search did not identify a direct systematic LLM paper on this exact
effect. That absence is not enough.

A weak result would be:

> "LLMs also show the human unpacking effect."

That is a human-bias replication, not the paper we are looking for.

It should only be reconsidered if later evidence suggests a qualitatively new
LLM-specific law — for example, an unexpected reversal, a sharp dissociation between
event equivalence recognition and probability invariance, or a causal mechanism that
predicts when unpacking matters across tasks.

**Decision:** keep below the observation/sampling-process candidate. No pilot.

# 6. Pilot authorization gate — tightened

A candidate may enter **SMALL PILOT** only after all six pass:

1. **Measurement reality** — no ratio instability, pseudo-replication, hidden
   item dependence, or selection artifact.
2. **Behavioral plausibility** — a result could in principle generalize across
   modern families and more than one real semantic substrate.
3. **Abstraction test** — remove our terminology and state exactly what a reader
   learns.
4. **Novelty assassination** — actively find the papers that would compress it
   into an existing phenomenon; include current 2026 work, not only older ACL
   papers.
5. **Scale test** — plausible C1→C2→C3 path, not "benchmark + accuracy."
6. **Simple-data test** — Figure-1-level examples, 2–4 natural conditions,
   direct gold.

**Current result:** no candidate has yet passed all six.

# 9. Current one-sentence principle

> **A classic distinction is allowed; a classic conclusion is not.**

The substrate may be old and simple. The paper earns novelty only by discovering
a new, non-obvious, stable LLM-specific law and then explaining it deeply.
