# 3. CK — human common-knowledge theory does NOT kill the candidate

## RQ

> **When first-order facts are matched, does public observability cause LLMs to treat recursive knowledge as a qualitatively compressed common-knowledge state rather than merely another finite level of nested Theory of Mind?**

## Why the new PNAS paper helps

De Freitas, Huang & Pinker (PNAS 2026) argue that humans combine:
- capacity-limited recursive mentalizing;
- public salience, which licenses arbitrarily deep common knowledge.

They directly compare private / reciprocal / doubly reciprocal / public information.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13486529/

This is **not** a novelty kill. It gives CK:
- a mature scientific object;
- clean competing hypotheses;
- human reference behavior;
- exact diagnostic conditions.

## What would be too weak

> “LLMs also rate public events as more common-knowledge-like.”

That is a replication.

## What could be Main-level

Examples of LLM-specific laws that the human paper does not itself establish:

1. **finite-to-common overclosure regime:** some model/training families prematurely treat finite reciprocal evidence as common knowledge;
2. **reasoning/use dissociation:** a model can answer explicit nested-belief questions correctly but still uses a publicness shortcut in downstream coordination;
3. **training-regime split:** reasoning training increases finite recursion depth while leaving publicness compression unchanged—or changes the two in opposite directions;
4. **representation dissociation:** public/common-knowledge state is distinct from ordinary finite ToM state and has selective causal consequences.

These are examples of possible law shapes, not claims to be forced.

## Main-vs-Findings gate

CK remains a pilot because the actual model law is unknown.

KILL / demote if the result is only:
- generic ToM depth degradation;
- public/private recognition;
- exact reproduction of the human curve;
- one lexical public-announcement cue;
- one-family curiosity.

## Verdict

> **SMALL PILOT AUTHORIZED / REOPENED AS TOP EXISTING COGNITIVE CANDIDATE / NOT MAINLINE**

The old CK-P1 design remains provenance and should be recalibrated to include human-vs-model law analysis rather than treated as invalid.

# 4. New Candidate EPM — Perspective-Relative Epistemic Modality

## Research Question

> **When a sentence says that something “might” or “must” be true, can an LLM evaluate that modal relative to the relevant agent's information state, or does privileged narrator/model knowledge leak into the judgment?**

## One example

Actual world:
- the ball is red.

Alice:
- has not looked in the box;
- knows the ball could be red or blue.

For Alice:

> “The ball might be blue.”

is epistemically licensed, although blue is not the actual state.

The model must distinguish:

```
objective truth
≠
what is compatible with Alice's information
```

## Paper Identity

**Focused scientific / behavioral discovery** at the intersection of formal semantics and social cognition.

Mechanism is optional initially.

## Scientific axis

Existing modal benchmarks usually test modal force / logical validity.

Existing ToM benchmarks usually test what an agent believes/knows.

EPM asks whether **epistemic modal meaning is anchored to the correct information holder**.

```
same world
+
same proposition
+
same modal vocabulary
+
different agent-indexed information state
→
different licensed modal judgment
```

## Competing hypotheses

**H1 — Perspective-sensitive modality**  
`might/must` is evaluated against the designated agent's compatible worlds.

**H2 — Omniscient truth leakage**  
actual narrator truth contaminates the modal judgment.

**H3 — Model-self anchoring**  
the LLM evaluates against all information available in the prompt/model rather than the named agent.

**H4 — Explicit-ToM / modal dissociation**  
the model can correctly state what Alice knows but still evaluates Alice's `might/must` incorrectly.

## Direct neighboring work

- conditional/modal LLM benchmarks already study epistemic modality;
- CogToM / other ToM work studies subjective belief states;
- recent semantics work emphasizes that epistemic modals depend on a contextually relevant body of knowledge;
- the MODUS methods pilot asks whether modal expressions maintain content-specific alternatives, but explicitly frames itself as exploratory methodology rather than this agent-perspective question.

References:
- https://xchuan-li.github.io/research/modus/
- https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1448/
- https://academic.oup.com/book/55106/chapter/423915369
- https://academic.oup.com/book/58147/chapter-abstract/480293328

## Reviewer kill sentence

> **“This is just modal reasoning × Theory of Mind.”**

This is the key assassination target.

EPM survives only if the decisive interaction cannot be reduced to either parent:
- modal-only competence cannot solve which information set supplies the modal base;
- ToM-only belief tracking does not test whether natural-language modal semantics reads that information state.

## Why it matters

Agent assistants routinely need to say:
- what **I** know is possible;
- what **the user** could reasonably consider possible;
- what **another agent** must infer from partial evidence.

Collapsing these perspectives can produce false certainty, false contradiction, or bad coordination even when first-order facts and beliefs are individually tracked correctly.

## Minimal substrate

Very shallow:
- boxes/cards/schedules/simple observations;
- 2–3 agents with explicitly controlled access;
- exact possible-world gold;
- same proposition / world truth matched across perspective conditions;
- no external knowledge;
- no judge.

## Outcome robustness

Interesting outcomes include:
- correct perspective-relative anchoring;
- systematic reality leakage;
- model-self anchoring;
- explicit-ToM/modal dissociation;
- reasoning-vs-conversational family split;
- asymmetry between `might` and `must`.

## What would be trivial

- model knows `might` means uncertainty;
- model knows Alice did not see the box;
- prompting “from Alice's perspective” improves accuracy;
- formal modal-logic formulas are solved.

## Verdict

> **SERIOUS NEW LEAD / NO PILOT YET**

Next gate: dedicated search through modal-language, perspectival semantics, and ToM datasets to ensure no direct agent-indexed modal benchmark already owns the axis.

# 5. New Candidate HWP — Hypothetical-World Persistence / Modal Subordination

## Research Question

> **When language introduces a merely possible or hypothetical world, does an LLM maintain and update that world as a distinct discourse state, or does information leak between the hypothetical and actual worlds?**

## One example

> A wolf might walk into the garden. It would growl at the dog.

The second sentence is naturally interpreted **inside the hypothetical wolf-world**. It does not assert that a real wolf is currently growling.

A stronger matched test can later introduce:
- facts true only in the hypothetical world;
- actual-world facts that conflict with them;
- later references that should selectively retrieve one branch.

## Paper Identity

**Scientific / behavioral discovery** grounded in dynamic semantics, with a natural bridge to model-based simulation and planning.

## Scientific object

```
actual discourse state
≠
hypothetical discourse state
```

and the key question is **persistent branch-specific update**, not word-level knowledge of `might/would`.

## Direct neighbors

Formal semantics has a long literature on modal subordination. A 2025 ACL workshop paper continues to develop formal treatment, but is not an LLM behavioral study.

General LLM world-model / counterfactual work is broad, but the current search did not locate a modern LLM paper that makes **natural-language hypothetical branch persistence vs world leakage** its load-bearing object.

References:
- https://aclanthology.org/2025.brigap-1.3/
- https://aclanthology.org/2016.lilt-14.2/
- https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.507.pdf

## Competing hypotheses

**H1 — Branch-preserving discourse model**  
hypothetical facts remain available to later subordinate discourse but do not contaminate actual-world answers.

**H2 — Hypothetical→actual leakage**  
facts introduced under `might/if/would` are later treated as actual.

**H3 — Actual→hypothetical overwrite**  
known actual facts prevent the model from maintaining a conflicting hypothetical branch.

**H4 — Local lexical scope only**  
the model handles one modal sentence but loses the branch across discourse distance/anaphora.

## Decisive prediction

Hold entity/content constant. Manipulate whether a fact is introduced:
- actually;
- hypothetically;
- in a subordinate continuation.

Then query both worlds separately.

A real branch-sensitive computation predicts selective persistence and selective non-transfer.

## Why it matters

This is relevant to:
- hypothetical planning;
- scenario analysis;
- counterfactual discussion;
- fiction/simulation;
- agent reasoning about alternative future plans.

A system that cannot keep hypothetical facts quarantined can corrupt memory or make invalid future-action inferences.

## What would be trivial

- model recognizes `if`;
- one-sentence counterfactual accuracy;
- next-token probability differs after `might`;
- generic world-state facts are decodable.

## Main-level growth path

Potential coherent story:
- C1: characterize world-leakage law;
- C2: show branch persistence / leakage generalizes across modal, conditional, and planning language without turning into a benchmark zoo;
- optional C3: identify/selectively intervene on branch-specific state if a mechanistic paper becomes justified.

## Verdict

> **SERIOUS NEW LEAD / NO PILOT YET**

Dedicated assassination is still required against counterfactual-world-state and narrative/discourse tracking papers.

# 6. FC — Free-Choice Inference, re-evaluated under Alignment-First

V9 kept this as secondary search only.

Alignment-first calibration makes it somewhat more credible because ACL 2026 Best **Imperfective Paradox** shows that a classic formal-semantic puzzle can support a high-level LLM scientific paper when it reveals a structural bias.

Classic free-choice pattern:

> You may take an apple or a pear.

typically licenses:
- you may take the apple;
- you may take the pear.

This is not a straightforward theorem of ordinary modal logic, and current formal-semantics work still actively debates the mechanism.

Recent human/theory references:
- https://doi.org/10.3765/sp.19.7
- https://academic.oup.com/jos/article/41/1/1/7643965

A direct LLM search found:
- broad pragmatic competence work;
- modal/deontic benchmarks;
- an ICLR 2026 workshop ModalBench containing deontic paradoxes including Ross/Chisholm.

Thus:

> **“Can LLMs solve free-choice examples?” is too small.**

The only serious shape is a structural discovery paper asking whether models reproduce the linked **free-choice / dual-prohibition / ignorance / modal-flavor** pattern or instead show a stable non-human inference regime.

## Verdict

> **SERIOUS SEARCH LEAD / NO PILOT**

It needs stronger assassination and a claim architecture before compute.

> **Does the evidential value of exactly the same chosen action depend on what alternatives were genuinely available?**

- raw chosen-item traces;
