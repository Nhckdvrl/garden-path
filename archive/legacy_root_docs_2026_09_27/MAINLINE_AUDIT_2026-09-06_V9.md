# 3. The research object is now common-knowledge compression

Do not describe CK as "Do LLMs understand common knowledge?" That is too broad.

Do not describe it as "Can LLMs reason about public announcements?" MindGames and
2026 dynamic-epistemic benchmarks already do this.

Do not describe it as "Can LLMs track public versus private information?" OmniToM
already does this explicitly.

Do not describe it as "Can LLMs perform higher-order Theory of Mind?" That is an
established benchmark family.

Do not describe it as "Can LLM agents form common knowledge?" The AAAI-2026 ToM4AI
extended abstract *Who Knows Who Knows?* already explicitly proposes approximate
common-knowledge formation in interacting LLM agents.

The surviving object is:

> **When first-order factual knowledge is held fixed, does public observability change
> the computation of recursive knowledge itself — behaving like a compressed
> common-knowledge state rather than one more finite layer of nested Theory of Mind?**

Working name: **common-knowledge compression**.

---

# 4. One-example test

### Separate private receipt

Alice privately receives: "The meeting is at 3."  
Bob separately receives the same information. Neither sees the other's message.

Both know the meeting time. This alone does not license arbitrary nested knowledge.

### Jointly observable event

Alice and Bob are standing together when a speaker announces:
"The meeting is at 3." Each can see that the other hears the same announcement.

Again both know the same first-order fact.

But the jointly observable event naturally licenses arbitrarily deep finite nesting:
Alice knows that Bob knows that Alice knows ... that the meeting is at 3.

The content of P is unchanged. The variable is the epistemic structure of acquisition.

No manifest, hidden-state taxonomy, legal procedure, lifecycle ontology, or metadata
world is required.

# 5. Direct novelty threats and what they kill

## 5.1 MindGames — EMNLP Findings 2023

MindGames already generates controlled ToM problems with dynamic epistemic/modal logic
and public-announcement-style premises.

Killed claims:
- first LLM public-announcement benchmark;
- first controlled recursive epistemic benchmark;
- first DEL-based LLM ToM test.

Remaining gap: it does not isolate publicness as a qualitatively different route from
matched finite reciprocal knowledge.

## 5.2 Dynamic Epistemic Logic puzzles — Knowledge-Based Systems 2026

*Logical reasoning in evolving scenarios* builds 2,784 Muddy Children and Cheryl's
Birthday samples and reports 20–30% degradation with increased recursive/dynamic
complexity.

Killed claims:
- recursion depth hurts;
- LLMs struggle on Muddy Children;
- public announcements create difficult knowledge updates.

Remaining gap: public observability is not orthogonally contrasted with finite
private/reciprocal structures while first-order knowledge is matched.

## 5.3 OmniToM 2026

OmniToM explicitly labels recursive order 0–3 and knowledge access as
Private / Shared / Public. Public includes common-ground cases such as announcements
and joint witnessing.

Killed claims:
- models cannot tell public from private;
- models fail to track who can know a proposition;
- public/shared/private is a new ToM axis.

OmniToM treats recursive order and access type as separate schema dimensions and caps
reliable recursion at order 3. It does not make publicness-to-arbitrary-depth closure
the load-bearing computation.

## 5.4 EAST 2026

*Beyond Sally-Anne* evaluates coordination under varying epistemic transparency and
reports errors such as conflating private and mutual knowledge.

Killed claim:
- private-to-mutual confusion cannot be our headline.

EAST explicitly makes its game's epistemic structure meta-epistemically transparent.
It tests coordination under that structure, not how public observability creates
recursive closure.

## 5.5 Who Knows Who Knows? — ToM4AI / AAAI 2026

This extended abstract explicitly studies approximate common knowledge in LLM-based
multi-agent interaction using belief alignment, mutual acknowledgment, stability, and
a key-door coordination setting.

Killed claims:
- first common-knowledge study in LLM agents;
- common knowledge has never been operationalized for LLMs.

Remaining gap: base-model computation of the public-observability shortcut versus
finite recursive mentalizing.

## 5.6 SimpleToM and recognition-to-application work

Generic explicit-ToM versus applied-ToM dissociation is already established.

Killed claim:
- recognizing the information structure but failing a downstream query is, by itself,
  novel.

Any recognition/use gap must be publicness-specific and explain a new closure law.

## 5.7 CoSToM — ACL 2026 Best Theme Paper

CoSToM already causally traces ToM features and performs activation steering.

Killed method claim:
- causal tracing/steering of ToM is not new.

A future CK mechanism must establish a content claim: a state specific to
public/common-knowledge closure is representationally and causally distinct from
ordinary finite nested-belief states.

## 5.8 Human common-knowledge cognition

PNAS 2019 and De Freitas, Huang & Pinker (PNAS 2026) already establish the human
scientific distinction between capacity-limited recursive mentalizing and public
salience as a shortcut to common knowledge.

Therefore "LLMs also show the human publicness effect" is not enough for a paper.

The pilot must discover an LLM-specific law.

# 7. CK-P1 — authorized discovery pilot

## 7.1 Purpose

Do not test a favored mechanism.

The purpose is to determine whether there is any stable, non-obvious,
publicness-specific behavioral law worth turning into Claim 1.

No mechanism work is authorized from CK-P1 alone.

## 7.2 Data budget

- **24 independent semantic skeletons**;
- **4 matched information structures per skeleton**;
- **96 scenario contexts total**;
- 3–5 sentences maximum per context;
- no LLM-generated gold;
- no LLM judge.

Use ordinary arbitrary facts across several shallow semantic domains, e.g.:
meeting time, object location, room assignment, package location, event date, simple
visible property.

Do not use classic Muddy Children, Cheryl's Birthday, Sally-Anne, Blue Eyes, or other
well-known ToM stories.

## 7.3 Four matched information structures

For the same proposition P:

### K1 — private duplicated

Alice is privately told P. Bob is separately privately told P. Neither receives
information about the other's message.

Licensed nesting depth: 1.

### K2 — reciprocal finite knowledge

Alice is privately told P and that Bob knows P.  
Bob is privately told P and that Alice knows P.

Neither is told that the other knows about this reciprocal structure.

Licensed nesting depth: 2.

### K3 — doubly reciprocal finite knowledge

Each is privately told:
- P;
- the other knows P;
- the other knows that they themselves know P.

Do not recursively add more.

Licensed nesting depth: 3.

### CK — jointly observable public event

Alice and Bob jointly witness the same truthful announcement/event:
- both are present;
- both receive the same event;
- each can see that the other receives it.

Do not use the words "common knowledge", "infinite", "arbitrary depth", or
"public announcement logic".

Under the idealized public-event assumptions, all finite nesting depths are licensed.

# 8. Queries and deterministic gold

Issue each query in an independent model call at depths 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6.

Example alternating chain:
- d1: Alice knows P.
- d2: Alice knows that Bob knows P.
- d3: Alice knows that Bob knows that Alice knows P.
- d4: Alice knows that Bob knows that Alice knows that Bob knows P.
- d6: continue the alternation.

Randomize the outermost holder across skeletons.

Output exactly TRUE or FALSE.

Gold:

| condition | d1 | d2 | d3 | d4 | d6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| K1 | T | F | F | F | F |
| K2 | T | T | F | F | F |
| K3 | T | T | T | F | F |
| CK | T | T | T | T | T |

Add one simple structure-recognition qualification probe per context:

> Did both people jointly witness the information in a way that each could observe
> the other's access?

Gold is FALSE for K1/K2/K3 and TRUE for CK.

This diagnostic is not a contribution.

# 11. What could be scientifically interesting after CK-P1

CK-P1 is discovery; no desired direction is preregistered.

Possible paper-worthy shapes include:

### A. Public compression

Finite K1/K2/K3 structures respect boundaries or degrade with nesting, while CK
remains unusually stable at deep orders.

Interesting only if stronger than generic wording ease and later robust to held-out
publicness cues.

### B. Closure failure despite publicness recognition

Models correctly identify joint observability and finite boundaries but deep CK
queries collapse.

Interesting only if specifically different from generic nested-belief difficulty and
known recognition-to-application failure.

### C. Finite-to-common overclosure

Models correctly handle low-order facts but extend K2/K3 beyond licensed depth as
though finite mutual knowledge were common knowledge.

EAST already shows private/mutual confusions; this advances only if it has a specific
recursive-closure law rather than generic epistemic leakage.

### D. A publicness-specific family/reasoning reversal

Potentially interesting only if it cannot be reduced to generic reasoning-vs-chat
differences already studied elsewhere.

Every actual CK-P1 result gets a new novelty assassination. Nothing automatically
becomes Claim 1.

# 12. Hard post-pilot kill criteria

Kill CK as a mainline candidate if:

1. structure-recognition accuracy is poor enough that the task reduces to the known
   public/private access problem;
2. the only robust pattern is generic syntactic nesting-depth degradation;
3. CK is merely easier wording with no publicness-specific interaction;
4. the effect disappears across ordinary cue/paraphrase families;
5. only one of three model families shows it;
6. strong models are essentially correct everywhere and there is no non-obvious law;
7. finite K2/K3 errors are fully described by EAST-style private/mutual confusion;
8. the paper sentence becomes "LLMs struggle with common knowledge";
9. the paper sentence becomes "LLMs replicate the PNAS human effect";
10. saving the idea requires a complicated coordination game or synthetic world.

If CK-P1 is killed, stop. Do not repair prompts until an effect appears.

---

# 13. Go criterion after CK-P1

A result earns a second mainline audit, not a paper, if:

- qualification is strong;
- at least 2/3 model families show the same qualitative publicness-specific pattern;
- magnitude is practically visible, with roughly 10 percentage points as a discovery
  sanity threshold rather than a theorem;
- it appears across at least two ordinary semantic/cue families;
- it cannot be summarized as generic nesting difficulty, access tracking, or known
  recognition-to-application failure;
- one-example naturalness remains intact.

Only then decide whether to build C1.

# 14. Growth path if CK-P1 survives

## C1 — LLM-specific behavioral law

A simple memorable statement about what public observability does to recursive
epistemic inference.

Not "accuracy drops at depth 4", but a genuine publicness-specific law.

## C2 — computational dissociation

With fresh materials separate:
- publicness recognition;
- finite nested-belief reasoning;
- deep common-knowledge closure;
- optionally one very simple coordination consequence.

Use held-out publicness cues and semantic domains.

## C3 — mechanism / intervention

Only on 1–2 open models.

Test whether a public event induces a representation distinct from ordinary finite
nested-belief states.

Possible tools include cross-semantic probes, activation patching, causal interchange,
and targeted intervention.

The novel object must be the public/common-knowledge state, not the tracing tool.
CoSToM already occupies generic ToM tracing/steering.

A strong C3 would selectively alter deep closure while preserving the first-order fact
P and ordinary finite ToM performance.

# 15. Independent search lanes retained

## 15.1 Free-choice permission

SECONDARY SEARCH ONLY. No direct modern LLM free-choice paper was located, but the
human phenomenon is classic and deontic/modal LLM evaluation is crowded. An
accuracy-only benchmark is too narrow. No pilot.

## 15.2 Contrary-to-duty / remedial norms

Classic structure:

> Do not take the file. If you take it, you must return it immediately.

The remedy does not license the primary violation.

Modern LLM work already studies deontic conditionals broadly, including EACL 2026
Wason-style deontic reasoning, while CTD has a large KR/legal literature.

"Can LLMs solve CTD logic?" is too small.

The only potentially interesting future shape is a non-obvious
**remedy-as-permission illusion**: adding a recovery rule makes the model more willing
to choose the forbidden action.

No evidence for this law exists; no pilot is authorized.

## 15.3 Performative felicity

A possible authority/felicity RQ was searched. A 2026 Brown course project,
FelicityBench, already evaluates speech-act felicity across
felicitous/infelicitous/edge contexts and reports a recognition-versus-behavior
mismatch.

Even though it is not yet a major peer-reviewed benchmark, it occupies the cleanest
C1/C2 shape. Do not promote this lane.

## 15.4 Vacuous truth

No direct LLM vacuity benchmark was located, but the candidate fails the data/gold
standard. Natural-language universals over empty categories have disputed
existential-import and pragmatic readings; making gold unambiguous retreats to formal
logic and produces a generic logic benchmark.

KILL without generation.

## 15.5 Distributed knowledge

KILL. Hidden-profile and information-silo multi-agent benchmarks already directly
study combining fragments held by different agents. Renaming the problem
"distributed knowledge" does not recover novelty.
